# UI/UX Editorial

Start with the reader's question and the evidence that answers it. Structure a page as proposition → concrete product surface → capabilities/use cases → proof → clear next step. Omit sections that add no information. Do not invent customer claims, readiness, benchmark numbers or working features.

Use restrained navigation, a decisive heading and one clear primary action. Alternate generous editorial space with dense product or technical detail. Align sections to a consistent grid; numbered sections and compact monospace labels can aid orientation when they carry meaning. Avoid all-caps paragraphs and gratuitous technical decoration.

For catalogs, use consistent image ratios and aligned labels with clear object identity. Use original/licensed media, meaningful crops, alt text and stable fallbacks. Cinematic media belongs where it explains a product; avoid autoplay audio and provide reduced-motion/static alternatives. Keep text legible across all media frames.

Show product UI as evidence at a readable scale. Preserve its actual proportions and label illustrative states. Do not apply large promotional headlines, full-bleed video or empty cinematic spacing to an everyday workspace; route that surface to [workbench](../ui-ux-workbench/SKILL.md).

## Deliver
Provide section outline with a purpose per section, grid and type hierarchy, responsive media plan, real content/assets inventory and primary-action path. Review desktop and narrow layouts, especially heading wraps, image crops and CTA clarity. A visually impressive page without product comprehension fails review.
